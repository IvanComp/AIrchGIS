Porto Recanati, Potentia Sud

43.412373°, 13.671348°

0.24 ha (80x30 m)

Interpretation

Roman road and necropolis (type 8).

Topography

Situated in the valley bottom, directly southwest of the presumed circuit wall of Potentia.

Investigation

Identified (2001) and since then regularly monitored through aerial photography; targeted survey (2002)

and geo-archacological research (2003-2004). This area was completely surveyed with a magnetometer in

2012; revisited and confirmed in 2015. The remote sensing data allow us to identify a clear road structure

(ca. 5 m wide) leading from the area of the southem gate of Porentia in a SW direction to the arca of the

former Roman bridge (site 133), Both sides are in this area flanked by a series of traces (of which at least

6 are seen as large rectangular cropmarks), interpreted in analogy with those al site 106 (northern

necropolis) as funerary monuments and some surrounding individual burials. Augerings by the P'VS team

indicated that the roadbed was artificially elevated above the flood plain.

Artefacts

Long zone of gravel mixed with pottery and building debris corresponding with the "road and

monuments” visible on aerial photographs, The building material consists of fragments of white

limestone, some pieces of concrete, tegulac, a piece of marble and some sandstone. The pottery consists of

amphorae (Dr 2-4, LRA Affican type) and some finer wares (coarse and plain wares, TS, ARS), It is

situated mainly in a zone of 30x10 m parallel with the Roman road. Artefact density: 279.2/ha (116);

8.S/ha (off-site).

Interpretation

Roman road and necropolis (npe 4).

Topography

Situated in the valley bottom, directly west of the circuit wall and the western gate of Potentia,

Investigation

Identified through intensive (random) survey in 2002, followed by magnetic prospection in 2005 and

2012; the whole zone has been object of systematic acrial photography from 2001 onwards and the nearby

westerm gate and direct extramural area were excavated by the PVS team in 2007-2010, Intensive

gcoarchacological survey (corings, geo-clectric prospection) followed in 2013. The small concentration of

building materials found by artefact survey was the first indication of the presence of a large necropolis

extending from the western town gate to the still standing “Torraccio” structure, a concrete remainder of a

tower-shaped funcrary monument with square plan, located at some 600 m west of the ancient town wall.

Since then, the other surveys and our excavations confirmed this cemetery, in particular the intense

concentration of monuments and graves on both sides of the outgoing road to Rome, but also some burials

at greater distance from the transport axis. The Latter, well observed as crop mark in acrial photography,

was identified as a max, 6m wide gravel, at least locally flanked by ditches. Excavation showed that

immediately outside the gate it was enlarged into a kind of circulation plaza.

Artefacts

The artefact survey of 2002 showed that a higher density of gravel occurs near the NW comer of the field,

in an area of 10x10 m where a clear rectangular crop mark was also seen on acrial photos; an intense

concentration of limestone blocks (white and pink) occurs bere. Almost no pottery is associated, so it is

difficult to date this probable Roman building structure. Artefact density: 31.3/ha.
